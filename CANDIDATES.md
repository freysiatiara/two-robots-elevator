# Candidate lines — Two Robots in an Elevator

Panel drafted and scored in the builder session against the judge's five
criteria (in order): (1) reads as one funny line of awkward robot-to-robot
elevator small talk; (2) machine logic / sensors / firmware / charging shows
through the politeness; (3) the awkwardness lands — pause, forced courtesy,
the thing neither robot wants to name; (4) funny on first read, surprising
turn, not a tired pun; (5) tight single line, every word earning its place.
Target length: 80–110 characters. Tie-breaks favour fewer bytes.

## Head-to-head: B vs D (top two)

- (1) On-topic small talk: tie.
- (2) Machine voice: tie — logs vs charging cable, both unmistakably robot.
- (3) Awkwardness: **B** — the awkwardness is personal between the two robots (47 shared rides, never once asked), not just a shared pretense.
- (4) First-read surprise: **B** — auditing your own logs to discover you've been rude is a more original turn than offering a cable.
- (5) Tightness: **D** — 87 vs 99 chars; B wastes nothing either.

B wins on criteria 3 and 4, the higher-weighted ones after 1–2. D's byte
advantage (12 bytes) only matters on an exact rate tie, which B's stronger
criteria 3–4 should prevent.

## Chosen line (99 characters, 99 bytes)

> I checked my logs: we've shared this elevator 47 times and I've never once asked about your servos.
